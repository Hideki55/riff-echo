How it works

1Hear the riff
2Noodle until you find it
3Play it all the way through

Tap any bubble to hear that note on its own. Playing the first note starts a new attempt. Any octave counts, and a repeated note only has to be played once.

Instrument

Sets the note range and how the riff sounds. Voice riffs stay within one octave.

Sound

Tap a sound to hear it and use it. "Real" sounds are recordings of real instruments and download the first time you pick them (about 1 MB). Each instrument remembers its own sound.

Mood

Each mode has its own colour. The bars show how bright or dark it sounds.

BrightestDarkest

Style

Sets the riff's groove and the drum beat. The strips show a typical rhythm (one square = one 16th note).

Drum beat & volume

Pick the groove for each style. Each style has its own drum kit. Rows show kick, snare and cymbals over one bar.

Key

Neighbouring keys on the wheel share the most notes.

Riff length

Longer riffs are harder to remember.

Pitch tolerance

How close your voice must be to the note (100 cents = one semitone). Standard matches EarMaster's quarter-tone rule. Relaxed allows the 50–70 cents listeners usually tolerate in singing. Your pitch is averaged over each note, so vibrato is fine. The green zone on the tuning dial shows your setting.

Mic sensitivity

When you play, the level bar on the tuning dial should pass the red line. When you're quiet, it shouldn't.

Helpers

Make your own riff

Tap notes in order. Each tap plays the note. Your riff is saved with your other riffs.

Length of the next note or rest

Tap a block to change its length. Grey blocks are rests. Thin lines mark beats, thick lines mark bars. Swipe sideways for longer riffs.
